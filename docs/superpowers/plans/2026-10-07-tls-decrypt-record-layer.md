# JAM-204: TLS 1.3 Record-Layer Decryption Plan

## Reviewer-approved amendment (2026-10-07)

- **Late key-log lines:** Retain complete, not-yet-decryptable ciphertext in a per-direction key-wait state for up to 2 seconds of the injected capture clock, capped at 64 KiB per direction. These bytes count against the same 4 MiB TLS aggregate budget and active-direction/per-PID limits. When the matching key appears, replay retained bytes in TCP order. If the key is absent at timeout or the wait cap is reached, emit terminal `unavailable/no_key`, clear the bytes, and do not resume that direction.
- **0-RTT trial rule:** When ClientHello advertised `early_data` and the client handshake epoch has not started, trial-decrypt each client record once with the client handshake secret at sequence zero. A failed trial does not advance a sequence. The first authenticated handshake-key record starts that epoch. Cap the skip at 32 records or 256 KiB; exceeding either emits terminal `unavailable/early_data_unresolved` and clears the direction. Each record incurs at most one trial; early plaintext is never emitted.
- **Recoverability:** `no_key` is recoverable only during its 2-second key-wait window. `unsupported_cipher`, `handshake_not_observed`, `evicted_budget`, and `early_data_unresolved` are terminal `unavailable` reasons. `tcp_gap`, `capture_truncated`, `overlap_conflict`, `record_invalid`, and `record_overflow` are terminal `desynchronized` reasons. `close_notify` ends cleanly. Emit at most once per `(connection, direction, reason)`; only `no_key` may transition to recovery, and only before its deadline/cap.
- **Secret/ring ownership:** Transfer a key-log secret into the relevant direction state when consumed, then remove and zeroize the stored copy. Remove handshake secrets after that direction authenticates Finished and installs its application secret; remove the application secret after installing it into direction state. Derive KeyUpdate secrets in place. Per-PID direction-cap eviction selects that PID's least-recently-active direction and never another PID's. Keep one decrypted ring buffer per connection, tagging each stored entry and emitted payload with its direction; keep directional HTTP/2/HPACK state separate.
- **Additional tests:** Cover a key-log line arriving after encrypted handshake records, late-key timeout/cap, accepted and over-limit early-data trials, terminal/recoverable reason transitions, eviction isolation between PIDs, key removal after transfer/consumption, and the shared direction-tagged ring.

## Goal

Decrypt TLS 1.3 application data from explicitly opted-in processes only when records arrive in a contiguous TCP stream, authenticate with the negotiated cipher suite and correct directional traffic secret, and feed plaintext to direction-local HTTP/2 parsers. Never guess past a lost byte or failed authentication.

## Protocol and state model

Each connection has a client and server endpoint, established from the endpoint that sent ClientHello. If the capture did not observe enough of the handshake to establish that endpoint and the negotiated cipher suite, report `unavailable/handshake_not_observed`; do not infer direction from ports. Parse ClientHello, including the `early_data` extension, ServerHello cipher suite, and HelloRetryRequest followed by the second ClientHello and ServerHello. Continue using the same handshake's client random for key-log lookup.

Support only `TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256` initially. Its traffic secret uses HKDF-SHA256 and the existing AES-128-GCM primitive. Treat `TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384`, `TLS_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256`, and any other cipher suite as `unavailable/unsupported_cipher`, not as a record authentication failure. Add support for other suites only with independent vectors and review.

Store key-log secrets by `(pid, client_random, label)`, with labels kept distinct. For each direction, decrypt encrypted handshake records using the matching `CLIENT_HANDSHAKE_TRAFFIC_SECRET` or `SERVER_HANDSHAKE_TRAFFIC_SECRET`. Do not emit handshake plaintext. On an authenticated Finished message, switch that direction to its matching `_TRAFFIC_SECRET_0` and start its application-epoch record sequence at zero. Apply the bounded early-data trial rule in the amendment when ClientHello advertised `early_data`. Early plaintext is out of scope and is never emitted.

For every authenticated TLSCiphertext, derive the nonce as the directional static IV XORed with the 64-bit sequence number in the final eight bytes. Increment only after successful authentication. Strip zero padding and the final inner content-type byte from TLSInnerPlaintext. Skip plaintext compatibility ChangeCipherSpec records (outer type 20) without changing any sequence counter. An authenticated alert ends that direction cleanly; `close_notify` is not desynchronization. Parse handshake messages incrementally across records. A complete KeyUpdate authenticated under the current application secret advances only that direction to `HKDF-Expand-Label(secret, "traffic upd", ...)` generation `N+1`, then resets its record sequence counter to zero.

## TCP record reassembly, budgets, and desynchronization

Do not reuse `TcpReassembler` as the record byte stream: it is a 16 KiB decision-window prefix buffer that stops after a detector decision or two seconds. Add a dedicated sliding per-direction stream consumer that releases newly contiguous bytes as records are parsed. Anchor it to observed TCP sequence space; if the initial stream boundary or handshake is missing, report `unavailable/handshake_not_observed` rather than parsing at an arbitrary byte offset. Preserve wraparound-safe sequence comparisons and first-seen-wins overlap handling.

An RFC 8446 TLSCiphertext body is at most `2^14 + 256` (16,640) bytes; including the 5-byte record header, the maximum wire record is 16,645 bytes. Reject a declared body length above 16,640 as `desynchronized/record_overflow` before allocation. Permit one maximum wire record of out-of-order bytes beyond a gap, in addition to the current incomplete record; that is the explicit reordering window. If a missing sequence range remains past the gap-hold timeout, capture truncation is known, conflicting overlapping bytes are observed, or record framing is invalid, mark only that direction terminally desynchronized, clear its partial buffers, and stop decrypting it. The reason is that a lost byte makes record boundaries and AEAD sequence numbers unknowable. A forged on-path segment can therefore cause denial of decryption for that direction; accept this availability tradeoff and never surface unauthenticated plaintext.

Give TLS decryption a separate budget so opted-in traffic cannot evict JAM-170's L7 sniff buffers or be evicted by them. Initial caps: at most two maximum wire records per direction (current incomplete record plus one full record of reordering), 1,024 active directions, 64 active directions per PID, and 4 MiB total measured TLS byte-buffer capacity, including HTTP/2 buffers and HPACK dynamic-table allocations. The temporary late-key wait has its separate 64 KiB per-direction limit but remains inside these same aggregate and active-direction caps. Account vector capacity, clamp before resizing, and reject untrusted lengths before allocation. Evict least-recently-active TLS directions under pressure and report terminal `unavailable/evicted_budget`; per-PID caps evict only that PID's LRU direction. Never silently resume an evicted stream. Use the same injected `now_ms`/ReplayClock timeline for key-wait, gap-hold, idle, and eviction times.

Released TCP sequence ranges are treated as already delivered: a fully old retransmission is ignored, and a segment straddling the consumed edge contributes only its new tail. Do not retain another record of history solely to compare old retransmissions. Before consumption, conflicting overlaps inside the bounded reorder window still terminate with `overlap_conflict`; if first-seen bytes form altered TLS ciphertext, AEAD authentication fails closed. The endpoint likewise rejects altered ciphertext, so comparing already-consumed bytes would add memory and a denial-of-decryption signal without changing accepted plaintext. Plaintext CCS and failed early-data trials emit no content and do not advance a TLS record sequence; old retransmissions of those consumed TCP ranges are ignored by the same rule.

Capture metadata (`captured_len < original_len`) is passed through the parsed-packet path to terminate an eligible TLS direction with `capture_truncated`. Key-wait and pending-gap deadlines are checked before processing another packet and by the periodic timer, so a late packet cannot revive an expired stream and a quiet live capture still expires against its monotonic agent clock. Replay checks use the published capture-time clock.

## Lifecycle and secret handling

Make each key-log secret explicitly zeroizing (`Zeroize`/`ZeroizeOnDrop`) with a redacted manual `Debug`; do not derive debug output containing secret bytes. Keep secrets owned by the eligible PID, client random, and label. Bound stored secrets to 128 client-random sessions per PID and 1 MiB total, with a 10-minute TTL for sessions that never acquire a flow; evict oldest entries and zero them. On PID unregister, remove and zero that PID's secrets and all associated TLS, HTTP/2, and decrypted-ring state. On FlowTable `ConnectionClosed`, remove and zero the flow's secret entries and all per-direction state. Store raw secrets in zeroizing buffers and create transient AEAD/HKDF key material; document that `ring`'s internal key schedule objects do not expose a zeroization guarantee. Preserve the current best-effort mlock and zero-on-eviction behavior of `DecryptedRingBuffer`; no new key or plaintext disk path is permitted.

Keep decryption state per `(connection_id, direction)`, including a separate HTTP/2 reassembler/HPACK decoder, record parser, traffic-secret generation, sequence number, and bounded reassembly buffer. Keep one zeroized-on-eviction decrypted ring buffer per connection; tag each stored entry and emitted payload with its direction. Feed only authenticated application plaintext in order to that direction's HTTP/2 parser. Redact headers before any event is emitted.

## Status event and UI contract

Add an additive `decryption_status` event with `connectionId`, `direction` (`client_to_server` or `server_to_client`), `status` (`unavailable` or `desynchronized`), and a bounded `reason`. Reasons: `no_key`, `unsupported_cipher`, `handshake_not_observed`, `evicted_budget`, `early_data_unresolved`, `tcp_gap`, `capture_truncated`, `overlap_conflict`, `record_invalid`, and `record_overflow`. Apply the recoverability table in the amendment. Emit only for flows belonging to an eligible PID. This metadata contains no ciphertext, plaintext, or key material and is not subject to the decrypted-content rendering gate; `decrypted_payload` remains gated exactly as today. Add direction to decrypted-payload entries/events. Update Rust serialization, `docs/wire-protocol.md`, `lib/types.ts`, and `lib/agent-mapping.ts` together. Unknown future reason strings must be handled without throwing.

## Security invariants

- Decryption remains per-process opt-in through `bin/osi-inspect.js`.
- Only authenticated application plaintext reaches HTTP/2 parsing; handshake and early-data plaintext are not emitted.
- Missing bytes, unsupported suites, malformed records, or failed authentication never yield plaintext.
- Keys and transient plaintext are zeroized where the code controls their allocations; ring-managed plaintext remains best-effort mlocked and zeroed on eviction.
- Review follow-up: the vendored HPACK dynamic table now zeroizes entries on eviction, drop, and parser desynchronization; its allocated capacity counts against the TLS budget. Huffman output, partial decode results, owned Cow names on malformed-value errors, decoded byte pairs, and sensitive temporary header values are zeroized. Permanent HTTP/2 desynchronization clears the plaintext buffer and resets the HPACK decoder immediately. Terminal TLS directions also replace the handshake parser so its allocation is released and no longer counts against the global buffer budget.
- No key/plaintext is written to disk or included in status events; rendering stays behind `lib/decrypted-payload-gate.ts`.
